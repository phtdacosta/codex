## Governance

This is the project's **living dense layer**. The article changes only when the model's geometry changes; these references change when evidence, interpretation, objections, or date-sensitive science improve; the Research Program changes when an open problem or test changes.

Every entry carries **Article load-bearing?** If **No**, it does not migrate into the article unless the geometry changes. **Negative evidence, null results, uncertainty, and hostile sources belong here as first-class entries:** the project retains them rather than harmonizing them away. A new quotation is not progress unless it sharpens an entry's definition, boundary, status, or evidential weight.

The status labels are stable across the project:

- **Physical Anchor:** supported by contemporary physical science or observational astronomy.
- **Historical Witness:** genuinely attested in the named text, thinker, or tradition.
- **Structural Analogy:** resembles part of the proposed model without being identical to it.
- **Proposed Synthesis:** machinery introduced by this project itself.
- **Internal Challenge:** a source or argument retained because it pressures the model rather than supporting it.

Last reviewed for this version: **26 September 2026** (article version 2).

## A

### Admissible range {#admissible-range}

**Status:** Proposed Synthesis.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes.  
**Definition:** The set of concrete realizations that an antecedently selected parameter is understood to permit without specifying which realization will occur.  
**Role in model:** The range is the middle term that stops authorial parameterization from becoming either vacuous or scene-level. A subject must know more than a word such as *loss* while knowing less than a complete future scene.  
**Boundary:** An admissible range is not consent to every later event inside the range. Another agent's act is not transformed into the recipient's act merely because the recipient selected a broad vulnerability or exposure.  
**Main objection:** The theory does not yet specify how much information is sufficient for authorship. Too little knowledge empties the choice; too much recreates full-script selection.  
**Cross-links:** Authorial parameterization; Limited archetypal selection; Providential integration; Problem of evil.  
**Research status:** Open; Research §1.3 and §2.2.

### Adamic root agency {#adamic-root-agency}

**Status:** Historical Witness / Structural Analogy.  
**Article load-bearing?** No.  
**Definition:** The structural pattern in which an act near the root of human history establishes conditions inherited by later history.  
**Role in model:** Adam is the root case of the article's two channels: his choice was his own authorship, and what reached his descendants is influence, since original sin is "contracted" and not "committed" (CCC 404). Genesis also shows that a free act need not be repeated for its downstream consequences to become entrenched.  
**Boundary:** Genesis does not teach that every person premortally chose a life. Jewish readings of Genesis should not be collapsed into later Augustinian original-sin doctrine. Christian reception in Romans 5 and 1 Corinthians 15 intensifies the root-act pattern but still does not supply individual premortal parameter-selection.  
**Sources:** [Genesis 2-3 at Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.3?lang=bi); Romans 5:12-21; 1 Corinthians 15:21-22; [CCC 404](http://www.scborromeo.org/ccc/para/404.htm).  
**Cross-links:** Influence and authorship; Inherited consequence, not inherited guilt; Antecedent agency; Christian compatibility matrix; Myth of Er.

### Anattā / anātman {#anatta-anatman}

**Status:** Historical Witness / Internal Challenge.  
**Article load-bearing?** No.  
**Definition:** The Buddhist denial that the changing aggregates constituting ordinary experience amount to a permanent, independent self.  
**Role in model:** It pressures the strong version's assumption that one numerically continuous chooser must persist from antecedent agency through embodied life. Buddhism preserves causal and karmic continuity while resisting precisely the substantial identity the model naturally wants.  
**Boundary:** Anattā is not nihilism and does not mean that Buddhism denies all causal continuity, responsibility, or conventional personhood. Nor can Buddhist rebirth simply be translated into a Western soul migrating between bodies.  
**Sources:** [Saṃyutta Nikāya 22.59](https://suttacentral.net/sn22.59/en/sujato); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "The Buddha"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddha/).  
**Cross-links:** Personal identity; Soul-continuity; Karma; Buddhist cosmic cycles.

### Antecedence trilemma {#antecedence-trilemma}

**Status:** Proposed Synthesis.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes.  
**Definition:** The three-way choice among premortal created succession, atemporal creaturely agency, and ontological antecedence without a premortal episode.  
**Role in model:** It prevents the phrase *before time* from hiding three incompatible ontologies. Each horn preserves a different desideratum and incurs a different cost.  
**Horn 1:** Premortal succession best preserves intuitive creaturely authorship and creates the largest Christian-anthropological cost: read as souls existing before their bodies, it is contrary to Catholic teaching (Leo I, Letter 15; ST I q.118 a.3).  
**Horn 2:** Atemporal creaturely agency best preserves literal extra-temporality and creates the largest metaphysical burden concerning deliberation without succession.  
**Horn 3:** Ontological antecedence best fits classical theism but risks returning deepest authorship to divine actualization rather than creaturely act.  
**Boundary:** Aquinas's divine eternity is not evidence that created persons can deliberate timelessly. The article commits only to a choice and keeps the three horns open, as discussions of its mode.  
**Sources:** For a rare treatment of atemporal creaturely agency, Hope Sample, "Anne Conway's Atemporal Account of Agency," *Ergo* 9 (2022), DOI [10.3998/ergo.2259](https://doi.org/10.3998/ergo.2259).  
**Cross-links:** Premortal succession; Atemporal creaturely agency; Ontological antecedence; Divine eternity; Christian compatibility matrix.  
**Research status:** Core open problem; Research §1.2.

### Antecedent agency {#antecedent-agency}

**Status:** Proposed Synthesis.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes.  
**Definition:** Ultimate personal sourcehood placed prior to ordinary embodied temporal execution, where *prior* may be temporal, atemporal, or ontological depending on the horn of the antecedence trilemma. The article claims a choice, and that it was the person's own, and nothing about the state in which it was made: not a previous life, not a consciousness, not even the lack of one.  
**Role in model:** It relocates libertarian-like sourcehood away from every temporal decision point and into the conditions under which the world-history is instantiated. Temporal deliberation remains causally real inside the run. Temporal decisions are therefore genuine acts and genuine causes within execution, while their deepest sourcehood is antecedent rather than newly originated at each temporal decision point. *Decompression*, in the seed sense, names this relation: antecedent themes become concrete through the woven temporal run without turning each later decision into a second source of ultimate authorship. Since version 2 the article adds a small share of genuinely new in-run choice, the sliver, which is not decompression (see Frame and texture).  
**Boundary:** This is not ordinary compatibilism, standard Christian doctrine, Plato's exact theory, a claim about a previous life, or a result of physics. It is also not the claim that an agent chose every event.  
**Sources:** [SEP, "Free Will"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/freewill/). For a premortalist neighbor the article rejects, see Premortal consent theodicy.  
**Cross-links:** Authorial parameterization; Antecedence trilemma; Decompression; Frame and texture; Global historical determinacy; Personal identity; Origination regress / first-act problem.

### Archetype {#archetype}

**Status:** Historical Witness in Jungian psychology; reused within Proposed Synthesis.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, but only at the level of recurrent form.  
**Definition:** A recurrent structural form associated with typical human situations, capable of appearing through many different concrete images and events.  
**Role in model:** Archetypal language gives the model a vocabulary for the claim that unique surface events belong to a smaller family of existential structures. That vocabulary then helps explain how a limited parameter basis could generate diverse biographies and how one shared phase could manifest differently across people.  
**Boundary:** Jung does not provide a canonical twelve-item cosmic menu, and Jungian archetypes should not be equated one-to-one with planets. The project's parameter basis is not inherited from Jung.  
**Sources:** C. G. Jung, *The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious*, CW 9.i; [IAAP 2022 report on archetype theory](https://iaap.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Report-Archetype-Theory-Roesler-1.pdf).  
**Cross-links:** Parameter-basis problem; Constellation; Synchronicity; Celestial phase-semantics.

### Astronomical clock {#astronomical-clock}

**Status:** Physical Anchor.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, for the celestial extension but not for the freedom/determinacy core.  
**Definition:** The objectively calculable astronomical state of the solar system and local sky at a specified time and location.  
**Role in model:** The clock is a shared external temporal coordinate. A birth can define an individual astronomical reference state, while later configurations are shared phases generated by the same celestial mechanics. A calendar is a decoded clock: the Church computes Easter every year from the moon, as the first Sunday after the first ecclesiastical full moon on or after the vernal equinox, a rule traced to the Council of Nicaea (325).  
**Boundary:** The reality of an astronomical state does not imply that historical astrological meanings are true. Astronomy establishes the clock; semantics is a separate claim.  
**Sources:** U.S. Naval Observatory, ["The Date of Easter"](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/easter).  
**Cross-links:** Biblical calendrics; Genethlialogy; Celestial phase-semantics; Stars incline, they do not compel; Thermodynamic arrow.

### Atemporal creaturely agency {#atemporal-creaturely-agency}

**Status:** Proposed Synthesis / open metaphysical option.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, as one horn of the trilemma.  
**Definition:** A version in which a created subject performs the antecedent act without temporal succession.  
**Role in model:** It preserves the strongest literal reading of agency outside physical time.  
**Main problem:** Deliberation appears to involve ordered comparison, intention, and resolution. The burden is to explain how finite creaturely agency can possess such structure without succession.  
**Boundary:** Divine eternity in classical theism cannot simply be copied onto creatures.  
**Source:** Hope Sample, "Anne Conway's Atemporal Account of Agency," *Ergo* 9 (2022), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3998/ergo.2259).  
**Cross-links:** Antecedence trilemma; Divine eternity; Premortal succession; Ontological antecedence.

### Authorial parameterization {#authorial-parameterization}

**Status:** Proposed Synthesis.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes.  
**Definition:** The antecedent selection or acceptance of a high-level existential parameter together with a sufficiently understood range of admissible realizations, while leaving the concrete historical realization unspecified.  
**Role in model:** It replaces the morally and philosophically unstable phrase *premortal consent*.  
**Core condition:** known parameter → known admissible range → unknown concrete realization.  
**Interpretive note:** *Unknown concrete realization* means unknown to the antecedent chooser at the level of theme selection. Since version 2 the article also holds that the realization is not fully fixed in advance: within the range, it is shaped by the weave, by other agents and, in a small share, by the chooser's own sliver. The article's plainer word for a parameter is *theme*.  
**Boundary:** Parameterization is not blanket consent, scene-selection, moral absolution of other agents, or retrospective permission for any event that happens to occur. Decompression therefore cannot be read as the person's hidden selection of every later scene: providential integration, other agents, natural law, and inherited history remain part of the realized world.  
**Main objection:** The authorship threshold remains under-specified.  
**Contrast:** Premortal consent theodicy, which the article's moral locks reject.  
**Cross-links:** Admissible range; Limited archetypal selection; Problem of evil; Providential integration.  
**Research status:** Core open problem; Research §1.3.

## B

### Biblical calendrics {#biblical-calendrics}

**Status:** Historical/Textual Witness.  
**Article load-bearing?** No.  
**Definition:** Biblical use of celestial bodies to mark signs, appointed times or seasons, days, and years.  
**Role in model:** These passages establish that the biblical sky can work as a calendar and sign-system without establishing Hellenistic natal astrology.  
**Boundary:** Genesis 1:14 is not a blanket endorsement of divinatory astrology; biblical literature also contains polemics against astral divination and worship.  
**Sources:** Genesis 1:14; Psalm 104:19; [TheTorah.com, "What Is the Bible's Calendar?"](https://www.thetorah.com/article/what-is-the-bibles-calendar); [Radak on Genesis 1:14](https://www.sefaria.org/Radak_on_Genesis.1.14).  
**Cross-links:** Astronomical clock; Celestial signification; Magi.

### Buddhist cosmic cycles {#buddhist-cosmic-cycles}

**Status:** Historical Witness / Structural Analogy.  
**Article load-bearing?** No.  
**Definition:** Buddhist cosmological traditions describing world contraction, persistence, dissolution, and renewed expansion or formation over immense timescales.  
**Role in model:** These traditions offer a morphology of cosmic dissolution and continuation beyond the present world-system.  
**Boundary:** Buddhist cosmology is not an ancient prediction of modern heat death, statistical mechanics, dark energy, or general-relativistic cosmology.  
**Source:** [Dīgha Nikāya 27, Aggañña Sutta](https://suttacentral.net/dn27/en/sujato).  
**Cross-links:** Heat death; Anattā; Karma; One run or many.

## C

### Celestial phase-semantics {#celestial-phase-semantics}

**Status:** Proposed Synthesis / empirical extension.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, but only for the celestial extension.  
**Definition:** The hypothesis that a pre-specified astrological grammar can assign qualitative phase-descriptions to astronomical relations and that those descriptions correspond prospectively, above chance, to independently coded archetypal classes of lived events.  
**Role in model:** It makes astrology a decoder of the shared astronomical clock into a calendar of the qualitative seasons of a life, not a force that mechanically causes events. A calendar is kept for the most part and missed in places, so the hypothesis is probabilistic: above chance, with misses expected, as in Aquinas's "majority of cases" (see Stars incline, they do not compel).  
**Boundary:** No unknown planetary beam, quantum-entanglement analogy, or occult physics is required. Failure of the semantics does not falsify antecedent agency. The Church rejects the consulting of horoscopes and astrology as divination (CCC 2116); the extension is a hypothesis to test, never a practice.  
**Empirical burden:** Pre-registration, fixed rules, blinded event classification, appropriate null models, correction for multiple comparison, and resistance to retrospective reinterpretation.  
**Cross-links:** Astronomical clock; Plotinian signification; Stars incline, they do not compel; Ontos and ethos; Archetype; Jung's marriage-horoscope test.  
**Research status:** Testable extension; Research §4.

### Celestial signification {#celestial-signification}

**Status:** Historical Witness / Proposed Extension.  
**Article load-bearing?** No.  
**Definition:** The idea that celestial phenomena can indicate or signify terrestrial conditions without being the direct efficient cause of everything signified.  
**Role in model:** It allows a shared sky to function as a visible coordinate of an integrated world-history rather than as a set of external causal levers.  
**Historical witness:** Plotinus is especially important; Origen also resists astrological necessity while allowing discussion of signs.  
**Boundary:** Signification does not itself establish empirical correlation.  
**Sources:** Plotinus, *Ennead* II.3, [MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Plotinus/enneads.2.second.html); *Plotinus: The Enneads*, 2nd ed. (CUP, 2025), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009604987.018).  
**Cross-links:** Plotinian signification; Astronomical clock; Celestial phase-semantics.

### Christian compatibility matrix {#christian-compatibility-matrix}

**Status:** Proposed Synthesis / theological boundary.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, for claims that the system is Christianly reconstructible.  
**Definition:** A graded rather than binary assessment of compatibility.  
**Strong fit:** Classical theism, divine ontological priority, providential causal layering, distinction between primary and secondary causes.  
**Structural analogy:** Adamic root agency.  
**Non-conflict at explanatory level:** Christian eschatology need not equal a natural thermodynamic horizon.  
**Major reconstructive cost:** It depends on the horn. The article's minimal claim (a choice, with the chooser's state left open) carries no fixed anthropology. Horn 1, read as souls existing before their bodies, is contrary to Catholic teaching, which has held since Leo I that souls "did not exist until they were breathed into their bodies" (Letter 15) and teaches that each is created immediately by God (*Humani Generis* 36; CCC 366); Aquinas argues the same (ST I q.118 a.3). It falls outside the sixth-century anathemas, which condemned pre-existence joined to a pre-cosmic fall. Horns 2 and 3 are not ruled out by magisterial teaching.  
**Recommended label:** *A Catholic's speculation, not doctrine*, with each collision named where it occurs.  
**Sources:** Leo I, Letter 15 to Turribius (447), ch. 10, [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3604015.htm); Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.118 a.3, [text](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1118.htm); Pius XII, [*Humani Generis* §36](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_12081950_humani-generis.html); [CCC 366](http://www.scborromeo.org/ccc/para/366.htm).  
**Cross-links:** Hylomorphism; Primary and secondary causation; Adamic root agency; Christian eschaton; Antecedence trilemma.

### Christian eschaton {#christian-eschaton}

**Status:** Historical Witness.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, for the distinction between physical and theological endings.  
**Definition:** Christian claims concerning judgment, resurrection, transformed creation, and new creation.  
**Role in model:** It prevents natural physical exhaustion from being mistaken for the final metaphysical meaning of creation.  
**Boundary:** Christian eschatology is not a thermodynamic forecast and does not require the universe to naturally reach heat death before divine consummation.  
**Sources:** 1 Corinthians 15:35-57; Romans 8:18-25; Revelation 21-22; Isaiah 65-66.  
**Cross-links:** Physical Limit != Eschatological Resolution; Heat death; One run or many.

### Compossibility {#compossibility}

**Status:** Historical Witness / Structural Analogy.  
**Article load-bearing?** No, but conceptually central to the language of integration.  
**Definition:** Leibnizian concept concerning possibilities that can coexist within one possible world.  
**Role in model:** It clarifies why individually imaginable biographies cannot simply be combined. Antecedent contributions have to belong to one possible total history.  
**Boundary:** Leibniz's possibles never choose anything, and he does not teach persons contributing themes to a providential weave.  
**Sources:** Leibniz, *Discourse on Metaphysics*; correspondence with Arnauld; [SEP, "Leibniz's Modal Metaphysics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2025/entries/leibniz-modal/).  
**Cross-links:** Providential integration; No private fate; Constraint types.

### Constellation / archetypal activation {#constellation-archetypal-activation}

**Status:** Historical Witness in Jungian vocabulary / Structural Analogy.  
**Article load-bearing?** No.  
**Definition:** Jungian language for a complex or archetypal configuration becoming salient under particular circumstances.  
**Role in model:** Constellation language names latent structural potentials becoming manifest during a historical phase.  
**Boundary:** The project's proposed relation among antecedent parameters, celestial phase, accumulated history, and concrete events is not Jung's theory.  
**Sources:** Jung, CW 8 and CW 9.i.  
**Cross-links:** Archetype; Celestial phase-semantics; Synchronicity.

### Constraint types {#constraint-types}

**Status:** Proposed Synthesis.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes.  
**Definition:** The typed categories through which providential integration is analyzed: hard, soft, relational, permission/risk-envelope, forbidden, and nomic constraints.  
**Role in model:** They prevent all antecedent contributions from being treated as identical preferences and prevent the integration problem from collapsing into a vague utility maximization slogan.  
**Hard:** constitutive conditions not freely violable by the integrator.  
**Soft:** graded aims or exposures with multiple acceptable realizations.  
**Relational:** realizations requiring intersections among subjects.  
**Permissions:** classes of exposure accepted without choosing a particular event.  
**Forbidden:** logical or moral exclusions that aggregate benefit cannot legitimate.  
**Nomic:** stable physical regularities of the created world.  
**Boundary:** The categories name a future satisfaction semantics; they do not yet supply one.  
**Cross-links:** Providential integration; Problem of evil; Schematic notation.  
**Research status:** Research §2.1.

### Covenant of Alast {#covenant-of-alast}

**Status:** Historical Witness / Structural Analogy.  
**Article load-bearing?** No.  
**Definition:** Qur'an 7:172's scene in which Adam's descendants testify concerning divine lordship, later developed in Islamic interpretation as the primordial covenant.  
**Role in model:** The scene is a precedent for antecedent human-divine accountability without ordinary autobiographical recollection.  
**Boundary:** The verse does not say human beings selected biographies, parents, suffering, natal charts, or earthly scripts. Interpretations of the scene vary in literalness and metaphysical detail.  
**Source:** [Qur'an 7:172](https://quran.com/al-araf/172-177).  
**Cross-links:** Primordial gathering; Personal identity; Divine individuation; Myth of Er.

## D

### Decompression {#decompression}

**Status:** Proposed Synthesis (the author's term).  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes.  
**Definition:** The unfolding of antecedently chosen themes into the concrete scenes of a life, through the weave and the physical run. Decompression in the seed sense: a seed holds almost none of what it becomes, and what grows from it is built mostly from what comes from outside.  
**Role in model:** It names how a temporal decision can express the antecedent choice without being a second origin of authorship: insofar as a decision expresses the antecedent contribution, it is that contribution's concrete unfolding.  
**Boundary:** Not decompression in the archive sense, where a zip file restores a stored document: no scene is stored inside a theme. Providential integration, other agents, natural law and inherited history supply what the theme never contained. Decompression does not include the sliver, since genuinely new in-run choices are the one thing no seed contains (see Frame and texture). A decision made in response to someone else is real and is not the unfolding of one's own parameter.  
**Sources:** Jan Baptist van Helmont's willow experiment (published 1648): a 5 lb willow grew to about 169 lb in five years in 200 lb of dried earth that lost about 2 oz; see CSB/SJU Biology, ["Analyzing van Helmont's Classic Experiment"](https://employees.csbsju.edu/ssaupe/biol115/van_helmont.htm).  
**Cross-links:** Antecedent agency; Frame and texture; Procedural-generation analogy; Authorial parameterization.  
**Research status:** Research §2.2, task 7.

### Divine eternity {#divine-eternity}

**Status:** Historical Witness.  
**Article load-bearing?** No, but it governs the antecedence boundary.  
**Definition:** In classical theism, God's existence is not ordinary temporal succession.  
**Role in model:** It prevents the First Cause from being imagined as a physical state just before the Big Bang.  
**Boundary:** Divine eternity does not entail that finite creatures can deliberate or choose atemporally.  
**Sources:** Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.10; [SEP, "Thomas Aquinas"](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/aquinas/).  
**Cross-links:** Atemporal creaturely agency; Primary and secondary causation; Antecedence trilemma.

### Divine individuation / called by name {#divine-individuation-called-by-name}

**Status:** Historical Witness / Structural Analogy.  
**Article load-bearing?** No.  
**Definition:** Biblical motifs in which divine knowing, calling, naming, or vocation exceeds the subject's present autobiographical self-knowledge.  
**Role in model:** It supports the narrower theological thought that a person can be particular to God before the full biography is available from the creature's temporal point of view.  
**Key texts:** Isaiah 43:1; Isaiah 45:4; Isaiah 49:1; Jeremiah 1:5; Psalm 139:16; John 10:3; Luke 1:13 and 1:31; Revelation 2:17.  
**Boundary:** These passages do not establish premortal self-naming or parental premortal selection of a child's earthly phonetic name. Isaiah 43:1 addresses Jacob/Israel collectively; Psalm 139 is poetic rather than a technical doctrine of a scene-by-scene script.  
**Sources:** [Isaiah 43:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Isaiah%2043%3A1); [Isaiah 45:4](https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Isaiah%2045%3A4); [Isaiah 49:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Isaiah%2049%3A1); [Jeremiah 1:5](https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Jeremiah%201%3A5).  
**Cross-links:** Personal identity; Covenant of Alast; Adamic root agency.

## E

### Enūma Anu Enlil {#enuma-anu-enlil}

**Status:** Historical Witness.  
**Article load-bearing?** No.  
**Definition:** Major Mesopotamian celestial omen corpus correlating celestial and atmospheric phenomena with terrestrial outcomes, especially royal, political, collective, and meteorological conditions.  
**Role in model:** The corpus documents a pre-Hellenistic tradition of systematic celestial sign-grammar and helps trace the long historical development from shared sky to formal interpretation.  
**Boundary:** It is not equivalent to later natal astrology and does not establish empirical truth of its omens.  
**Sources:** Anne-Caroline Rendu Loisel, "Enuma Anu Enlil," *Encyclopedia of Ancient History*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781444338386.wbeah21126); Cambridge History of Science, ["Science and Ancient Mesopotamia"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-history-of-science/science-and-ancient-mesopotamia/C48D6E70188ED938863F479B692D465B).  
**Cross-links:** Old Babylonian eclipse omens; Genethlialogy; Astronomical clock.

### Eternal present {#eternal-present}

**Status:** Historical Witness.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, as the theology beneath the frame.  
**Definition:** Boethius's account of divine knowledge as an eternal present: God sees all temporal events, free choices included, at once, and this knowledge imposes no necessity on what is freely chosen.  
**Role in model:** The second answer to how the weave can hold while the sliver is genuinely open: the weave is made in view of every free choice without making any of them necessary. The first answer is the robustness of the frame (see Frame and texture).  
**Boundary:** It does not by itself explain how antecedent choices or the weave work. It is common ground for Thomist and Molinist schools and settles neither dispute; the article does not adopt middle knowledge.  
**Sources:** Boethius, *Consolation of Philosophy* V, prose 6; Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.14 a.13, [text](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1014.htm#article13).  
**Cross-links:** Frame and texture; Providential integration; Molinism; Divine eternity; Fate in Christian usage.  
**Research status:** Research §2.1, task 7.

### Evolution and the execution layer {#evolution-and-the-execution-layer}

**Status:** Physical Anchor / compatibility claim.  
**Article load-bearing?** No.  
**Definition:** The claim that cosmic, chemical, and biological evolution can belong inside the physical realization of the model rather than compete with its metaphysics.  
**Role in model:** It prevents antecedent agency from being tied to a special-creation chronology of modern human bodies.  
**Boundary:** It does not settle theological questions about Adam, original sin, monogenesis, or the spiritual soul. The same paragraph of *Humani Generis* that permits inquiry into the evolution of the body states that "the Catholic faith obliges us to hold that souls are immediately created by God" (§36).  
**Source:** Pius XII, *Humani Generis* §36, [Vatican text](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_12081950_humani-generis.html).  
**Cross-links:** Primary and secondary causation; Thermodynamic arrow.

## F

### Fate in Christian usage {#fate-in-christian-usage}

**Status:** Historical Witness.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, for the title's use of *fate*.  
**Definition:** The Christian reception of *fate* as the order of created causes carried out under providence. Boethius places providence in the divine mind and fate in changeable things; Aquinas adopts the definition ("a disposition inherent to changeable things"); and Augustine, whom Aquinas quotes, advises anyone who means God's will or power by *fate* to keep the opinion and correct the language.  
**Role in model:** It licenses "fate for the frame": the article uses the word in this Christian sense, for the woven order of themes and intersections, not for a power over God or for astral necessity.  
**Boundary:** Christian usage rejects fate as necessity imposed by the stars or as a power independent of God. Augustine advised avoiding the word; the article keeps the meaning and, knowingly, the word.

### Frame and texture {#frame-and-texture}

**Status:** Proposed Synthesis.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes.  
**Definition:** "Fate for the frame, freedom for the texture." The frame (the chosen themes, the weave of intersections, the arrow of time) is settled; if the celestial extension holds, the sky's calendar is a reading of that frame, not part of it. The texture (which realization each theme takes, which of its dates are kept) stays partly open to a small share of genuinely new choice during life: the sliver.  
**Role in model:** It replaces the version 1 claim that the physical run is "settled at the global level once initialized", keeping one actual history and a settled frame while affirming real in-run freedom. The author's images: a tire full of small defects still reads, rolling, as a perfect circle; the roughly one percent of the genome that separates us from the chimpanzee makes all the difference.  
**Boundary:** The sliver is small but real. It is not a percentage of causal credit divided between God and creature, since God is first cause of free choices without making them less free (ST I q.83 a.1 ad 3; [CCC 308](http://www.scborromeo.org/ccc/para/308.htm)). The robustness of the frame is a claim to defend, not a theorem.  
**Main objection:** Balance: a frame robust enough to absorb any sliver may make the sliver trivial, and a sliver strong enough to matter may break the frame.  
**Sources:** Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.83 a.1 ad 3, [text](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1083.htm).  
**Cross-links:** Global historical determinacy; Decompression; Eternal present; Ontos and ethos; Lazy argument; Determinism vs fatalism.

### Lazy argument {#lazy-argument}

**Status:** Historical Witness / Internal Challenge.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes.  
**Definition:** The ancient *argos logos* (*ignava ratio*): if an outcome is fated, acting to bring it about or prevent it is pointless. Chrysippus replied that outcomes are co-fated with the actions that bring them about.  
**Role in model:** The obvious objection to "fate for the frame". The article answers in two layers: the certainty of death has never made living pointless; and the themes land either way, while the version, led or dragged, belongs to the sliver. **Boundary:** The Stoic answer presupposes Stoic heimarmenē; the article's version keeps a real sliver of in-run freedom and a personal God.

### Myth of Er {#myth-of-er}

**Status:** Historical Witness / Structural Analogy.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, as the article's governing image; no, as evidence.  
**Definition:** Plato's closing myth in *Republic* X. Er, killed in battle, returns to tell what he saw (614b). ### Origination regress / first-act problem {#origination-regress-first-act-problem}

**Status:** Internal Challenge / Core metaphysical debt.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes.  
**Definition:** The challenge that relocating sourcehood to an antecedent act does not secure authorship if that act is itself sufficiently fixed by an earlier character or motivational profile; but removing sufficient prior determination raises the competing problem of luck, arbitrariness, or brute occurrence.  
**Role in model:** It pressures the word *sourcehood* itself. The theory needs more than an earlier location for choice; it needs an account of how the antecedent act can originate with the subject rather than merely transmit a still earlier determination.  
**Dilemma:** prior sufficient character → regress of sourcehood; no sufficient prior determination → luck/arbitrariness problem.  
**Required distinction:** reason-giving explanation must be distinguished from sufficient determination if the project is to defend an act that is both intelligible and originating.  
**Historical witnesses / challenges:** Augustine's *City of God* XII.6–7 refuses an ordinary efficient cause for the first evil will and treats its causality as deficient; *De libero arbitrio* II–III presses the origin of voluntary defection. Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* III.5, argues that voluntary acts help form states of character, which makes the first character-forming act an especially sharp question. Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.63, a.5, is a qualified witness only: he explicitly denies that the devil sinned in the first instant, though angelic agency still complicates any equation of created succession with ordinary bodily clock-time.  
**Boundary:** Augustine's deficient-cause analysis concerns the first evil will as defection, not a general theorem that every free act lacks an efficient cause. Aquinas does not supply a creature sinning in the first instant and does not prove atemporal creaturely origination. Historical precedents do not solve the contemporary control/luck problem.  
**Sources:** Augustine, [*City of God* XII.6–7](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120112.htm); Augustine, *De libero arbitrio* II–III, with Book III text at [The Logic Museum](https://www.logicmuseum.com/wiki/Authors/Augustine/De_libero_arbitrio/L3) and modern edition overview at [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/augustine-on-the-free-choice-of-the-will-on-grace-and-free-choice-and-other-writings/078B0C65D23789318CD663A7A36C4DC1/listing); Aristotle, [*Nicomachean Ethics* III.5](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html); Aquinas, [*Summa Theologiae* I, q.63, a.5](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1063.htm); [SEP, "Incompatibilist (Nondeterministic) Theories of Free Will"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/incompatibilism-theories/).  
**Cross-links:** Antecedent agency; Authorial parameterization; Personal identity; Antecedence trilemma.  
**Research status:** Core open problem; Research §1.5.

**Boundary:** Episodic memory is not sufficient as a criterion. The antecedent choice is not remembered, and since the article claims nothing about the chooser's state, it is not even clear that there was anything to forget; ordinary memory gaps therefore prove nothing either way.  
**Sources:** [SEP, "Locke on Personal Identity"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/locke-personal-identity/); [Personal Identity and Ethics](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/identity-ethics/); Hronich (2024).  
**Cross-links:** Soul-continuity; Divine individuation; Antecedence trilemma; Anattā.  
**Research status:** Core open problem; Research §1.1.

### Physical Limit != Eschatological Resolution {#physical-limit-vs-eschatological-resolution}

### Plotinian signification {#plotinian-signification}

### Prayer and providence {#prayer-and-providence}

**Status:** Historical Witness.

### Premortal consent theodicy {#premortal-consent-theodicy}

**Status:** Internal Challenge / neighboring model.  
**Article load-bearing?** No.  
**Definition:** Premortalist responses to the problem of involuntary suffering, which look to a choice or consent before birth to explain the sufferings of a life. Andrew Hronich's article examines this family of views.  
**Role in model:** Kept as a contrast: any theodicy that explains a person's suffering by their prior consent is what the article's moral locks reject.

### Premortal succession {#premortal-succession}

**Status:** Proposed Synthesis / open metaphysical option.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, as one horn of the trilemma.  
**Definition:** A created order in which subjects exercise antecedent agency through some form of succession before ordinary embodied physical history.  
**Role in model:** It most directly preserves the intuition that the creature itself deliberates and selects.  
**Cost:** Conscious personal pre-existence is outside mainstream Christian anthropology and requires a positive account of what a premortal human subject is. In Catholic terms, a soul existing before its body is contrary to teaching (Leo I, Letter 15; ST I q.118 a.3), though it is not the fall-linked pre-existence condemned in the sixth century; the article keeps this horn only as a picture to discuss.

### Primary and secondary causation {#primary-and-secondary-causation}

**Status:** Historical Witness.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, for the Christian-theistic causal architecture.  
**Definition:** Thomistic distinction between God's universal ontological causal priority and the genuine causal activity of created things.  
**Role in model:** It prevents providence from being treated as a rival physical mechanism. Physical explanations remain genuine explanations inside creation. Aquinas extends the same double causation to the will: God moves voluntary causes without depriving their actions of being voluntary (ST I q.83 a.1 ad 3). The article accepts this and asks a different question, whether the creature had any part in the shape of what it was given.  
**Boundary:** Aquinas's First Cause is not a deistic first push and does not make Aquinas a modern physical determinist.  
**Sources:** Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.2 a.3, [text](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1002.htm#article3); q.22, [Providence](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1022.htm); q.83 a.1 ad 3, [text](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1083.htm); [SEP, "Thomas Aquinas"](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/aquinas/).  
**Cross-links:** Divine eternity; Christian compatibility matrix; Evolution and the execution layer.

### Problem of evil under integration {#problem-of-evil-under-integration}

**Status:** Core unresolved debt.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes.  
**Definition:** The question why a good providential integrator permits a particular harmful intersection when other admissible realizations may appear conceivable.  
**Role in model:** It remains after the theory rejects the crude claim that victims selected every harm. Typed constraints clarify the problem but do not answer it. Harms done with another person's sliver are that person's authorship and only influence for the victim, so the classical free-will defense applies to that share. The article sets Plato's "The blame is his who chooses: God is blameless" (*Republic* 617e) against Christ's "Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents" (John 9:3).  
**Boundary:** The model's moral improvement over scene-level consent is not itself a theodicy.  
**Cross-links:** Providential integration; Constraint types; Authorial parameterization; No private fate; Influence and authorship; Inherited consequence, not inherited guilt; Premortal consent theodicy.

**Role in model:** It is the bridge from many sources of agency to one actual history.

### Stars incline, they do not compel {#stars-incline-not-compel}

**Status:** Historical Witness.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes, for the celestial extension.  
**Definition:** Aquinas's view that the heavenly bodies act on bodily powers and never directly on intellect or will. **Role in model:** The historical form of the calendar hypothesis: a calendar most people keep for the most part, with misses expected and the wise able to outrun it.  
**Boundary:** Aquinas's mechanism is causal, an influence on bodies, where the article's is signification. The Church rejects the consulting of horoscopes and astrology as divination (CCC 2116); the article treats astrology as a hypothesis to test, never as a practice.  
**Sources:** Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.115 a.4, [text](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1115.htm); [CCC 2116](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7E.HTM).

### Determinism vs fatalism {#determinism-vs-fatalism}

**Status:** Philosophical distinction.  
**Article load-bearing?** Yes.  
**Definition:** Determinism says outcomes occur through a complete causal history; fatalism says an outcome occurs regardless of the intervening processes.  
**Role in model:** It explains why deliberation, medicine, persuasion, institutions, and effort remain causally real. Since version 2 the article speaks of fate only for the frame, and only in the Christian sense of the word: the themes land through whatever causes occur, never regardless of them, so the article is not fatalist in this entry's sense, and the texture stays partly open to the sliver. If a person is saved through treatment, treatment belongs to the causal history rather than being bypassed by fate.  
**Boundary:** Determinacy does not imply passivity or practical predictability. The lazy argument is answered on these terms (see Lazy argument).  
**Source:** [SEP, "Causal Determinism"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/determinism-causal/).

## Source-use Rules

1. **Historical existence is not empirical validation.** A technically sophisticated ancient astrological system proves that a grammar existed, not that it worked.
2. **Structural analogy is not doctrinal identity.** Adam, Alast, Er, Aquinas, Buddhism, and Jung perform different jobs.
3. **Science supplies anchors, not theological conclusions.** Thermodynamics and astronomy constrain the model; they do not prove primordial agency.
4. **Negative evidence remains in the dossier.** Jung's statistically insignificant marriage-horoscope material stays because the project must preserve evidence that pressures its preferred extension.
5. **Dates and live scientific claims are versioned here, not in the article unless geometry changes.** DESI and late-time cosmology are the obvious examples.
